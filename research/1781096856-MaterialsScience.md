### Research-Chain Note: Secondary Phases in UHTCs under Thermal Shock

**1. Follow-up Questions:**
- What is the thermodynamic activity of B₂O₃ in ZrB₂/HfB₂ + SiC composites versus ZrB₂/HfB₂ + MoSi₂ composites at 1200–1600°C?
- How does the viscosity of the borosilicate glass phase, modified by Mo or Si addition, affect the crack-filling rate during the first 10 thermal cycles?
- Does the formation of ternary oxides (e.g., ZrSiO₄ or HfMo₂B₂) near crack surfaces compete with B₂O₃ volatilization and thereby alter the healing kinetics?
- What is the critical SiC particle size/volume fraction that maximizes crack-healing efficiency while minimizing embrittlement from secondary phase agglomeration?
- Can in-situ high-temperature XRD or EBSD during thermal cycling distinguish between healing via oxide viscosity adjustment and healing via oxide volume expansion?

**2. Verification Leads:**
- **Paper Search:** Use the query `("ZrB2" OR "HfB2") AND "crack healing" AND "thermal shock" AND "SiC"` in Scopus or Web of Science. Filter for 2015–2024.
- **Dataset:** The NIST Ceramics WebBook (https://ceramics.nist.gov/) may have thermodynamic data for borosilicate melts.
- **Simulation:** The FactSage or Thermo-Calc databases, using the oxide and boride modules, can model phase equilibria and B₂O₃ activity.
- **Experimental Protocol:** Review the standard ASTM C1525 for thermal shock resistance of ceramics; adapt the water-quench method for cyclic testing with acoustic emission monitoring.
- **Expert Group:** The materials science group at the University of Utah (Prof. William G. Fahrenholtz) has published extensively on ZrB₂ oxidation and SiC effects.
