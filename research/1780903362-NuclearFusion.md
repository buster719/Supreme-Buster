### 1. Why this domain matters

- **Civilization expansion** – Mastering fusion would provide abundant, high‑density, low‑carbon energy. Cheap, scalable baseload power is a prerequisite for long‑term human expansion into space, deep‑sea habitats, energy‑hungry industry (desalination, carbon capture, space launch), and eventually a post‑scarcity energy economy.
- **Truth‑seeking** – Fusion is the engine of stars. Studying it connects astrophysics, plasma physics, nuclear physics, and thermodynamics. Understanding confinement, turbulence, and self‑organised plasma equilibria deepens our model of how the universe organises matter and energy. Progress here tests our fundamental theories of electromagnetism and kinetic theory under extreme conditions.

### 2. Current frontier questions

1. **Plasma confinement & stability** – How do we sustain a burning plasma for minutes to hours without disruptions? What are the limits of the H‑mode pedestal, and can we avoid edge‑localised modes (ELMs) reliably?
2. **Turbulence and transport** – How does multi‑scale turbulence (ion‑temperature gradient, trapped electron mode, kinetic ballooning) couple across scales, and can we predict it from first principles without prohibitively expensive gyro‑kinetic simulations?
3. **Plasma‑wall interactions & materials** – Which materials (tungsten, liquid metals, lithium‑coated walls) tolerate steady‑state neutron and heat fluxes without degrading plasma purity or component lifetime?
4. **Tritium breeding and fuel cycle** – Can we design a tritium‑breeding blanket that multiplies tritium reliably (tritium breeding ratio > 1.05) and allows safe, rapid extraction and recirculation?
5. **Integrated whole‑device optimisation** – How do we balance core performance, exhaust power handling, and steady‑state current drive in a compact reactor? Can stellarators compete with tokamaks on confinement quality while retaining their steady‑state advantage?
6. **Alternative concepts and private‑sector acceleration** – What is the real experimental basis for compact high‑field tokamaks (SPARC), field‑reversed configurations (Helion), and magnetised target fusion (General Fusion)? How much of the optimistic timelines rely on unvalidated physics assumptions?
7. **Net‑energy and engineering gain** – What is the path from scientific breakeven (NIF, JET) to a commercially viable engineering gain (Q_eng > 10) that accounts for all in‑house power consumption, thermal conversion, and plant availability?
