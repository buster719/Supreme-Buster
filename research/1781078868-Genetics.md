**3. Working answer:**
It is *likely* that joint modeling can recover *some* discriminatory power lost in within-family comparisons. The core hypothesis is that parental genotype data provides information about the expected null distribution of inheritance, allowing more precise estimation of transmission distortion and reducing noise. This is particularly relevant for complex traits where effect sizes are small. However, the recovery is probably partial and depends heavily on:
*   **Trait architecture:** Higher heritability and stronger effect sizes lead to greater recovery.
*   **Study design:** Proper ascertainment of families (e.g., affected offspring) is crucial.
