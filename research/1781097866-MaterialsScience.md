### **Follow-up Questions**
1.  What is the measured capillary pressure or wettability of B₂O₃ (or analogous borate glass) on the oxide scales formed on additively manufactured (PBF/DED) nickel-based superalloys or stainless steels?
2.  How does the anisotropic, columnar grain structure in PBF/DED parts influence the path and kinetics of oxygen diffusion along grain boundaries versus through interconnected porosity or microcrack networks?
3.  Can *in-situ* monitoring techniques (e.g., thermal imaging, photodiodes) during PBF/DED be correlated with post-build micro-CT or SEM to map the 3D connectivity of porosity and lack-of-fusion defects that could serve as capillary channels?
4.  In boron-containing environments (e.g., certain molten salt reactors or boriding processes), does grain boundary oxidation create a preferential path for boron penetration, or does it act as a barrier to boron migration?
5.  What are the critical temperature and stress regimes where B₂O₃ capillary flow into cracks becomes a more significant degradation mechanism than simple grain boundary oxidation embrittlement?
