**Working answer (with uncertainty):**
The gap is likely **critical** when the genetic architecture features strong **background-dependent effects** (epistasis, dominance) and **rare variant contributions**. In such cases, population-level associations (which average over heterogeneous backgrounds and LD structures) can misestimate causal effect sizes. Within-family designs control for much of this heterogeneity, making their estimates more accurate for these architectures but less powerful statistically. The gap is likely **negligible** for traits driven by many common, additive variants with consistent effects across genetic backgrounds, where population-level corrections (e.g., for LD) work well.
