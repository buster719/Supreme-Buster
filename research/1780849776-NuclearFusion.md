## 1. Why This Domain Matters for Truth‑Seeking and Human Civilization

Nuclear fusion is, in principle, the energy source of the stars. If harnessed on Earth, it promises:

- **Near‑limitless, high‑density, low‑carbon energy** – a potential cornerstone for a long‑term, large‑scale human civilization.
- **Fuel abundance**: deuterium from seawater, tritium breedable from lithium. No long‑lived actinide waste (unlike fission).
- **Safety**: no chain‑reaction runaway; plasma instabilities quench if control is lost.
- **Truth‑seeking**: plasma physics, extreme states of matter, magnetohydrodynamics, turbulence, and neutronics all push our fundamental understanding of non‑equilibrium systems and matter under extreme conditions.
- **Civilisation expansion**: removing energy poverty; enabling deep‑space propulsion (fusion rockets), heavy industry decarbonisation, and ultimately a Type I/II Kardashev foothold.

Failure to achieve practical fusion would also be a truth – it would force us to understand which physical/engineering bottlenecks are truly unsolvable at economic scale, sharpening our models of plasma and complex systems.

## 2. Current Frontier Questions (as of secondary LLM knowledge cutoff mid‑2025)

1. **Energy gain and net electricity**
   - Recent demonstrations (NIF ignition, JET record, ITER‑precursor experiments) have shown fusion energy output exceeding input energy to the fuel (Q > 1), but no facility has yet demonstrated sustained, net‑electricity‑output operation with a breeding blanket and tritium self‑sufficiency.
   - What is the real‑world engineering Q for a complete plant, including recirculating power, thermal conversion, and tritium extraction?

2. **Plasma confinement and stability**
   - Tokamaks face edge‑localised modes (ELMs), disruptions, and turbulent transport that degrade confinement.
   - Stellarators (Wendelstein 7‑X) offer steady‑state operation but are geometrically complex. Can optimised stellarator designs reduce turbulent transport enough to compete with tokamaks on Q?
   - How do we control burning plasma physics – self‑heating by alpha particles changes transport and stability in ways not yet fully tested.

3. **Materials and divertor life**
   - The reactor‑wall interface (divertor, first wall) must withstand intense heat flux (10–20 MW/m²) and a neutron flux that damages bulk material.
   - Advanced materials (tungsten‑alloys, liquid‑metal walls) are under test, but lifetime data under reactor‑representative conditions is scarce.

4. **Tritium self‑sufficiency**
   - A breeder blanket must generate enough tritium from lithium‑6 reactions. The breeding ratio (≥ 1.1) and the realistic off‑line fuel cycle with losses are still unproven at a scale relevant for a power plant.

5. **Alternative confinement / private‑sector concepts**
   - Inertial electrostatic confinement (Helion, TAE), magnetised target fusion (General Fusion), Z‑pinches (Zap Energy), and field‑reversed configurations are pursued with smaller, less‑capital‑intensive plants. Do they circumvent the tokamak/scaling issues, or do they face their own yet‑unpublished scaling cliffs?

6. **Economic viability**
   - Even if technical fusion works, will the levelised cost of electricity be competitive with fission, renewables + storage, or advanced geothermal by the time fusion plants can be built (2040s–2060s)? This requires whole‑system design optimisation that is rarely done publicly
