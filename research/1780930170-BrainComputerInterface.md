### Follow-up Questions
1. Does conveying per-trial neural signal quality (e.g., SNR, electrode impedance drift) alongside model doubt improve human trust calibration more than doubt conveyed only in natural language?
2. How does the temporal granularity of dynamic transparency (continuous uncertainty overlay vs. discrete doubt messages) affect BCI-assisted decision accuracy and over-trust in closed-loop motor rehabilitation tasks?
3. Does individual neural variability—such as differences in alpha-band power—moderate the benefit of dynamic doubt displays on trust calibration?
4. Would dynamic transparency about ignored signal features cause cognitive overload or alarm fatigue in long-duration BCI use, negating its benefit?
5. Can human subjects trained with dynamic doubt feedback transfer better trust calibration to a *different* BCI paradigm (e.g., P300 speller to motor imagery) than those taught with static explanations?
