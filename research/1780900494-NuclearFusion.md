## 1. Why It Matters
- **Energy abundance**: mastering controlled fusion would provide baseload power with fuel from seawater (deuterium) and lithium (tritium breeding), dramatically expanding Earth’s energy ceiling and enabling deep‑space missions.
- **Civilisation time/scale**: cheap, carbon‑free, high‑density energy could unlock large‑scale carbon removal, water desalination, and off‑planet habitats.
- **Truth‑seeking**: fusion plasmas are a natural laboratory for non‑linear dynamics, self‑organisation, magnetohydrodynamics, and particle‑wave interactions that approximate astrophysical environments. Understanding why the Sun’s corona is hotter than its surface or how accretion disks behave overlaps with fusion research.

## 2. Current Frontier Questions
- **Confinement time vs. instability**: how to sustain a burning plasma long enough to extract net energy while controlling edge‑localised modes (ELMs), disruptions, and turbulent transport (e.g., the “H‑mode” pedestal physics).
- **Tritium breeding**: a self‑sufficient fuel cycle requires a breeder blanket (e.g., LiPb, ceramic breeders) that reliably produces more tritium than consumed; neutronics and material compatibility are not yet demonstrated at scale.
- **First‑wall materials**: plasma‑facing components must withstand extreme heat, neutron bombardment, and helium embrittlement. Tungsten, beryllium, RAFM steels, and liquid metals are leading candidates, but lifetime data under reactor‑relevant conditions are sparse.
- **Integrated modelling and control**: real‑time magnetic control, disruption prediction, and fusion power output management with machine‑learning methods are still in early validation (e.g., DIII‑D, JET, KSTAR experiments).
