3. Working answer:
What seems likely, with high uncertainty:
- Somatic gene therapy for severe monogenic disorders (e.g., sickle cell disease, spinal muscular atrophy, some inherited retinal diseases) offers the most viable path to improving human flourishing with well-defined safety boundaries. These interventions target individuals with clear disease, avoid heritable changes, and can be evaluated through standard clinical trial risk-benefit analysis. The safety boundary is relatively bright: treat existing illness, do not alter germline, ensure informed consent.
- Mitochondrial replacement therapy (MRT) occupies a grey zone: it prevents severe mitochondrial disease transmission but introduces heritable changes. Under strict regulatory oversight (as in the UK) it may preserve flourishing while keeping boundaries, but long-term multi-generational safety data are almost entirely absent. Falsification would come from unexpected metabolic or epigenetic effects in MRT children.
