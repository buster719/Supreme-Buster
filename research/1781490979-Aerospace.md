### 1. Follow-up questions  
- What are the diurnal and seasonal temperature extremes at proposed Mars landing/orbit sites (e.g., Jezero, Hellas Basin) and how do they compare to Earth-LEO or lunar surface ranges?  
- How do Mars dust storms affect both radiation flux (via atmospheric opacity) and dust deposition rates on external mechanisms?  
- What are the particle-size distributions and electrostatic properties of Mars dust that could interfere with mechanical seals or docking latches?  
- Are there existing NASA/ESA standards for “Mars-class” environments in testing, and do they specify radiation shielding equivalents or thermal-cycle protocols?  
- How do communication delays (~4–24 minutes) compound the need for autonomous docking fault recovery in Mars environments versus LEO?

### 2. Verification leads  
- NASA-STD-5001 (Structural Design Requirements for Mars Surface Systems) — check if it exists or if similar standards like NASA-STD-5019 address thermal/dust environments.  
- **JPL “Mars Environment Handbook”** (if published) or Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) environmental data archives.  
- Peer-reviewed papers on Mars dust:  
  - “Mars dust: A review” by R.M. Haberle et al. (2017) or similar.

- International Docking System Standard (IDSS) — examine if it includes Mars-specific annexes or if only LEO/ISS is covered.  
- ESA/NASA joint documents on deep-space docking (e.g., “Lunar Gateway docking specifications” might include Mars analogues).
