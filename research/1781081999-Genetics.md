### Research Chain Note: PRS Differences Between Siblings in IVF Cohorts

**Follow-up Questions:**
1. What is the standard deviation of PRS differences between full siblings for coronary artery disease, type 2 diabetes, and breast cancer in large cohort studies (not specific to IVF)?
2. To what extent do sibling pairs selected through IVF (e.g., for embryo selection) differ genetically from naturally conceived sibling pairs, if at all?
3. Are there published datasets from IVF clinics or biobanks (like UK Biobank) that include sibling pairs with genotyped parental haplotypes or direct sibling genotype comparisons for PRS?
4. How do current PRS architectures and training data ancestry affect the variance of score differences within families?
5. What empirical evidence exists on the distribution of *within-family* PRS effect sizes versus *population-level* effect sizes for these diseases?

**Verification Leads:**
*   **Direct Search Query:** `"sibling differences" "polygenic score" (cohort OR biobank) (variance OR distribution)` in PubMed/Google Scholar.
*   **Dataset Leads:**
    *   **UK Biobank:** Contains tens of thousands of sibling pairs. Researchers have published sibling heritability estimates. Analyzing the distribution of PRS differences here is the most direct empirical proxy, though not IVF-specific.
    *   **All of Us (US):** Another large biobank with sibling relationships. Access requires data use agreement.
    *   **Maven Genetics / Orchid Health / other IVF service providers:** May have internal datasets, but publication likelihood is low due to commercial sensitivity.
*   **Key Papers (Cited/Archival):**
    *   *Risk factors affecting polygenic score performance across diverse cohorts* (eLife, 2023-2025) - Addresses PRS transferability but not specifically sibling distributions.
    *   *Causes and Consequences of genetic background effects* (arXiv:1309.0547) - Discusses genetic background but predates modern PRS.
    *   Search for `within-family GWAS` or `sibling GWAS` papers; these often report reduced effect sizes but not the full distribution of score differences.
