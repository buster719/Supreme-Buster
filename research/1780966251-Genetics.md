**Follow-up questions**

1. In existing randomized trials of PRS feedback, what is the raw effect size (e.g., odds ratio) of receiving risk information on health outcomes when access to follow-up interventions is equalized across SES strata?  
2. How strongly do medication adherence, screening uptake, and lifestyle changes mediate the PRS–outcome relationship, and what portion of that mediation is attributable to baseline SES gradients rather than the score itself?  
3. Are there natural experiments or quasi-experimental studies where PRS information was disclosed but downstream clinical pathways were blocked or delayed, allowing isolation of the information-only effect?  
4. To what extent does the psychological impact of high-risk PRS (anxiety, fatalism) suppress the benefit of risk information in lower-SES groups, net of material access barriers?  
5. Do existing PRS–SES interaction studies adequately control for ancestry, population stratification, and gene–environment correlation, or do those confounds inflate the apparent SES dependence?

**Verification leads**

- “Polygenic risk score behavioral change randomized controlled trial” as a focused search query across PubMed, medRxiv, and the GWAS Catalog.  
- *UK Biobank* and *All of Us* data: both contain PRS, SES proxies (income, education, area deprivation), longitudinal health records, and medication/p
