## Research-chain note: PGT-P gain measurement in sibling rank context

**Follow-up questions:**
1. Are there any published studies directly comparing PGT-P embryo selection outcomes using sibling rank correlation versus population percentile shift as the primary metric?
2. What is the typical correlation between polygenic scores of full siblings, and how does this constrain the maximum possible rank reordering from selection?
3. How do existing PGT-P simulation models (e.g., Karavani et al. 2019, Turley et al. 2021) report results—do they provide sibling-conditional distributions or only population statistics?
4. What would be the expected gain in median sibling rank (e.g., moving from 3rd to 2nd among 5 embryos) for a trait with heritability ~0.5 and sibling genetic correlation ~0.5, given a realistic prediction accuracy?
5. How does the reliability of PGT-P scores vary across the sibling distribution—does accuracy differ for top-ranked versus middle-ranked embryos?

**Working answer (high uncertainty):**
The expected gain in sibling rank from PGT-P selection is likely modest and diminishes rapidly with number of embryos. For a trait with heritability ~0.5 and typical PGT-P prediction accuracy (R² ~0.1-0.15), the correlation between true genetic value and PGT-P score is ~0.3-0.4. Among full siblings (who share ~50% of segregating genetic variants), the genetic value correlation is ~0.5 (for highly heritable traits).
