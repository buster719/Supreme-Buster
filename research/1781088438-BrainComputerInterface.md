### 1. Follow-up Questions

1. In BCI paradigms where the decoder adapts its decoding model without explicit user notification, do users report lower trust or higher discomfort after discovering the adaptation compared to systems that announce adaptation events?
2. Does the severity of trust erosion differ when covert adaptation produces *incorrect* inferences versus *correct* inferences that happen to work, or is the deception itself the primary driver?
3. How does "calibrated transparency" (telling users the system adapts but not how) compare to full disclosure and full concealment in maintaining trust over extended BCI use sessions?
4. In hybrid BCI-robot platforms, does overtly labeling the AI as "adaptive assistant" before interaction inoculate against later trust drops when the AI's behavior shifts?
5. Are trust drops from covert adaptation modality-dependent — i.e., does it matter whether the adaptation is in signal decoding, feedback presentation, or task scheduling?
