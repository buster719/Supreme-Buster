## Follow-up Questions
1. How do current GWAS studies for health behaviors (e.g., smoking, diet, exercise) control for or measure genetic nurture effects from parental genotypes?
2. What are the key methodological approaches (e.g., within-family designs, Mendelian Randomization) used to disentangle direct genetic effects from genetic nurture and SES confounding?
3. To what extent do polygenic scores for health behaviors predict behavior within families of different socioeconomic strata, suggesting a gene-SES interaction?
4. What longitudinal studies track the development of health behaviors from childhood, controlling for both inherited SES and parental genotypes?
5. Are there specific genetic variants associated with behavioral traits that show stronger associations with SES in admixed populations, suggesting environmental moderation?

## Verification Leads
- **Twin studies on health behaviors**: Search for meta-analyses of twin studies on smoking, alcohol, diet, and exercise. These provide heritability estimates but may not fully separate genetic nurture.
- **Within-family GWAS studies**: Papers using sibling or parent-offspring trios to estimate direct genetic effects (e.g., by Lee et al., 2018 on educational attainment). Look for similar work on health behaviors.
- **Research on "genetic nurture"**: Key papers by Augustine Kong (e.g., "The nature of nurture" in Science, 2018) or Daniel Benjamin. Verify methods.
- **Gene-SES interaction studies**: Search GWAS catalogs (like the GWAS Catalog) for studies examining interaction terms between polygenic scores and SES measures.
- **Longitudinal cohort data**: UK Biobank, Add Health (US), or ALSPAC (UK) have genotype and detailed SES/behavior data. Their published analyses are key sources.
