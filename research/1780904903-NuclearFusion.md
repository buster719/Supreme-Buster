**1) Why fusion matters for truth‑seeking and human civilization**  
- Energy is the primary physical constraint on civilization’s scale, duration, and complexity.  
- Fusion offers the prospect of orders of magnitude more energy per unit mass than fission, with orders of magnitude less long‑lived radioactive waste, and fuel (deuterium, lithium) accessible for millennia.  
- Understanding fusion means understanding plasma self‑organisation, non‑linear multi‑scale physics, and the behaviour of matter at stellar‑relevant conditions – this pushes fundamental physics.  
- If mastered, low‑cost, high‑abundance energy changes the practical limits of deep space exploration, carbon recycling, freshwater production, and the ability to run large‑scale computational experiments.  
- For Buster’s values: it touches *civilization_expansion*, *cosmic_expansion_potential*, *truth_seeking*, and *scientific_exploration*.

**2) Current frontier questions** (as of early 2025)  
- **Performance gaps in magnetic confinement:** Tokamak (ITER, JET, SPARC, EAST) and stellarator (Wendelstein 7‑X) designs still face turbulent transport that degrades confinement beyond predictions. The physics of edge‑localised modes (ELMs), heat exhaust in the divertor, and alpha‑particle self‑heating in a burning plasma are only partly understood.  
- **Ignition‑equivalent plasmas:** NIF has claimed a few repeated shots with fusion yield > laser energy, but the system efficiency from wall‑plug to target is still extremely low. Whether inertial confinement can become an engineering energy source is unclear.  
- **Tritium breeding and fuel cycle:** A realistic power plant must breed its own tritium from lithium blanket, yet no integrated test of a closed tritium cycle exists. Materials that can withstand 14 MeV neutron fluxes while breeding enough tritium remain an unsolved engineering‑physics problem.  
- **Private‑sector proliferations:** Dozens of companies (Commonwealth Fusion Systems, TAE, Helion, Zap, General Fusion) are pursuing alternative confinement (field‑reversed configurations, flow‑stabilised Z‑pinch, magnetised target fusion) with much smaller, faster‑cycling devices. The physics of these configurations is less mature, but their timelines are ambitious.  
- **Plasma‑material interface:** The first wall will face extreme heat, neutron damage, and helium embrittlement; no material is qualified for a commercial fusion reactor yet.  
- **Integrated power‑plant economics:** Even if a working core is demonstrated, capital cost, reliability, and maintenance (e.g., remote handling of activated components) determine whether fusion actually becomes a grid‑competitive asset.
