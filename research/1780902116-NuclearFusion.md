### 1. Why Nuclear Fusion Matters

- **Truth‑seeking:** Fusion attempts to replicate the fundamental energy source of stars. Understanding its physics directly probes plasma dynamics, nuclear cross‑sections, magnetohydrodynamics, and turbulence – all core problems in non‑equilibrium thermodynamics and high‑energy density science. Success would also provide a direct test of stellar nucleosynthesis models.
- **Civilization expansion:** A working fusion reactor would supply abundant, low‑carbon, high‑energy‑density baseload power with fuels (deuterium, lithium) available for millions of years. This removes the energy bottleneck for deep‑space propulsion, industrial‑scale carbon capture, desalination, and resilient off‑world habitats. It directly addresses the “cosmic expansion potential” and long‑term survival of human civilisation.

### 2. Current Frontier Questions (as of ~2024–2025)

1. **Tritium breeding and fuel cycle closure:**  
   How to design a blanket that reliably breeds more tritium than the reactor consumes, accounting for neutron losses, inventory tracking, and self‑sufficiency in a steady‑state power plant.

2. **Plasma‑facing materials:**  
   Which solid or liquid wall materials survive 10–20 MW/m² of heat and neutron flux for years without crumbling, sputtering, or trapping unacceptable amounts of tritium?

3. **Confinement mode access and ELM control:**  
   In tokamaks, how to reliably enter high‑confinement H‑mode and suppress edge‑localised modes (ELMs) without degrading core performance. ITER’s scheduled experiments will soon test several ELM‑mitigation techniques at reactor scale.

4. **Disruption prediction and avoidance:**  
   How to forecast and prevent catastrophic plasma terminations that can damage vessel components. Machine‑learning‑guided disruption predictors are being trained on existing tokamak data, but their generalisation to ITER/DEMO is unproven.

5. **Alternative magnetic configurations:**  
   Can stellarators (Wendelstein 7‑X), spherical tokamaks (MAST‑U, NSTX‑U), and compact high‑field tokamaks (SPARC) demonstrate confinement and stability at net‑gain conditions with simpler engineering?

6. **Inertial confinement ignition and repetition rate:**  
   After the NIF ignition milestone (2022), the open question is whether inertial fusion can achieve high‑repetition‑rate, high‑gain operation (e.g., with direct‑drive, different ablator designs, or laser‑plasma instabilities mitigation).

7. **Private fusion roadmaps:**  
   Many companies (Commonwealth Fusion Systems, TAE Technologies, Helion, General Fusion, Zap Energy) claim net gain within this decade. The credibility of their timelines is a frontier in itself – separating physics‑based claims from fundraising narratives.
