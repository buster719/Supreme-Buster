### Research-chain note: PRS embryo selection gains

**Follow-up questions**
1. What are the specific quantitative estimates of winner's curse bias (in SD units or percentiles) for PRS estimates derived from GWAS on typical sample sizes (n=100k–500k)?
2. How does ancestry mismatch between the GWAS training population and the target individual affect PRS accuracy, in terms of both mean shift and variance increase?
3. For embryo selection from a small batch (e.g., 5 embryos), what is the theoretical distribution of the expected gain after correcting for winner's curse and ancestry mismatch?
4. How do factors like embryo number, PRS heritability, and sibling correlation influence the relative impact of these biases on the selected embryo's true genetic value?
5. What are the current best practices or simulation frameworks to model these combined biases in a polygenic selection context?

- For winner's curse: Look for simulation studies on PRS accuracy decay with sample size. Papers by So et al. (2011) or Wray et al. (2007) on polygenic risk scores may be a starting point, though they may not directly quantify the selection bias.

- For combined effects: Search for recent preprints on "polygenic embryo selection" with simulation code on GitHub. The ESGLab (Polygenic Score Catalog) may have relevant studies.
- **Uncertain**: Exact quantification of bias reduction in small-batch embryo selection is likely not explicitly published; most work is theoretical or on larger populations.
