# Research-Chain Note: Australian Mitochondrial Donation Patient Eligibility

---

## 1. Follow-up Questions

- Has the NHMRC published final "Mitochondrial Donation Licensing" operational guidelines, or is the eligibility framework still embedded in the *Mitochondrial Donation Law Reform (Maeve's Law) Act 2022* and subordinate instruments?
- Does the NHMRC (or its Mitochondrial Donation Advisory Committee) maintain an enumerated list of qualifying mitochondrial diseases analogous to the UK HFEA's approved conditions list, or does eligibility rest on a clinical-genetic criteria threshold (e.g., mutation heteroplasmy level) without named diseases?
- Is there a publicly available "mitochondrial donation clinical trial protocol template" or licensing application form that specifies disease-specific eligibility criteria?
- How does the Australian approach compare to the UK's clinical pathway—are there conditions Australia covers or excludes that the UK does not, and vice versa?
- Were the eligibility criteria defined during the 2022–2023 NHMRC consultation process, and are the consultation submissions public?

## 2. Verification Leads

- **Primary legal instrument:** *Mitochondrial Donation Law Reform (Maeve's Law) Act 2022* (Cth). Search AustLII or Federal Register of Legislation directly.
- **NHMRC licensing framework:** Search NHMRC website for "mitochondrial donation licensing guidelines" or "mitochondrial donation advisory committee terms of reference." The NHMRC was tasked under Maeve's Law with developing licensing details. **Uncertain whether final guidelines have been published or are still in development.**
- **NHMRC public consultations:** Check consultations.nhmrc.gov.au for 2022–2024 consultation papers on mitochondrial donation eligibility.
- **UK HFEA comparison:** The HFEA maintains a published list of approved mitochondrial conditions (mitochondrial donation treatment conditions). This is a useful cross-reference: https://www.hfea.gov.uk
