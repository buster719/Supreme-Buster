**Follow-up questions:**
1.  In cohort studies with linked genetic, electronic health record, and socioeconomic data (e.g., UK Biobank, All of Us), what is the average proportion of the PRS-outcome association (e.g., for coronary artery disease, type 2 diabetes) that is attenuated when adjusting for medication adherence, screening uptake, and lifestyle factors?
2.  Does the magnitude of mediation by health behaviors differ significantly across strata of baseline socioeconomic status (e.g., education, income, neighborhood deprivation)?
3.  To what extent are the observed health behaviors themselves downstream of genetic predisposition (i.e., part of a genetic nurture or indirect effect) versus independently shaped by SES?
4.  Are there specific genetic variants or pathways where the mediation effect is notably stronger or weaker, suggesting a direct biological effect less amenable to behavioral modification?
5.  What statistical frameworks (e.g., mediation analysis with gene-by-environment interaction, counterfactual models) are most appropriate to decompose the PRS-outcome relationship into direct, indirect (via behaviors), and SES-confounded pathways?
