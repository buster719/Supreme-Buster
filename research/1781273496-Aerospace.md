### Follow-up questions
1. What are the exact published SEE and TID threshold values for the MIL-STD-1553 bus transceiver integrated circuits (ICs) commonly used in spacecraft, and how do these compare to the thresholds for SpaceWire physical-layer link controller ICs?
2. How do radiation-hardness requirements for standardized interfaces differ between ESA's ECSS-Q-ST-60-15C (components for spacecraft) and the U.S. MIL-PRF-38534 (hybrid microcircuits) specifications?
3. What are the failure modes (e.g., latch-up vs. functional interrupt) and associated mitigation strategies (e.g., current limiters, scrubbing) specific to each interface standard in a mixed radiation (SEE + TID) environment?
4. To what extent do the radiation-hardness specifications for the interface *protocol* (e.g., MIL-STD-1553 command/response structure) differ from those of the physical *implementation* (the ICs and cabling)?
5. Can the observed SEU rates in FPGA-based protocol controllers (like those for SpaceWire) be reliably predicted from component-level heavy-ion test data, or are system-level effects (e.g., cross-talk, power-supply noise) dominant?
