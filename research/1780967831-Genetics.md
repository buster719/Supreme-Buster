### 1. Follow-up questions

1. What is the empirical ratio of within-family PRS variance explained to population-level variance explained across major PGT-P traits (e.g., height, cognitive ability, T2D)?  
2. How does the shrinkage in PRS discrimination within families translate into clinical decision‑making metrics such as NNT (number needed to treat) or positive predictive value?  
3. Under what genetic architectures (LD structure, rare variant contribution, dominance, epistasis) does the within-family / population-level gap become negligible vs. critical?  
4. When PGT‑P is used for embryo selection, what is the expected gain in terms of sibling rank correlation rather than population percentile shift?  
5. Can joint modelling of parental genotypes and transmission patterns recover part of the discriminatory power lost in within-family comparisons?

---

### 2. Verification leads

- **UK Biobank** sibling/twin subsets: within‑sibship PRS R² for traits like height, BMI, educational attainment can be compared directly with population‑wide R². Several papers by the “within‑family GWAS” community (e.g., Howe et al. 2022, *Nature Genetics*; Young et al. 2022, *Science*; Okbay et al. 2022, *Nature Genetics*) are likely sources, but I cannot confirm the exact R² ratios without reading them.  
- **iPSYCH / Danish registers**: trios for psychiatric PRS discrimination within families.  
- **Simulation:** use `fmsim` or `pysimr` to generate realistic LD patterns and simulate selection from sib pairs with varying heritability and polygenicity; quantify the within‑family R² / population R² ratio.  
- **GTEx / expression data:** if part of the gap is due to non‑additive variation, within‑family PRS may still capture additive effects while population‑level PRS picks up population stratification; compare within‑family PRS from stratified vs. non‑stratified GWAS.  
- **PGT‑P clinical reports:** review actual PGT‑P cycle data (e.g., from Genomic Prediction, Orchid, or academic preimplantation studies) for real‑world sibling rank statistics, if publicly available.

### 3. Working answer (high uncertainty)

Within‑family PRS typically explains far less variance than population‑level PRS. For height, for instance, population PRS R² may reach ~15‑20 %, while within‑sibship R² commonly falls to ~5 % or lower. The gap arises because population‑level PRS conflates true genetic signal with ancestry
