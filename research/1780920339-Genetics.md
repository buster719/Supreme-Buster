# Research chain note — PRS utilization, socioeconomic gradients, and inequality

**Source: LLM secondary; untrusted.**

## 1. Follow-up questions
1. What is the observed magnitude of SES gradients in PRS-based testing uptake in existing genetic cohorts (UK Biobank, All of Us, FinnGen), and does it change when PRS are offered direct-to-consumer vs. clinical care?  
2. Do “tiered access” models in other stratified health technologies (e.g., imaging, NIPT, cascade screening) actually narrow utilization gaps, or do they primarily shift the point of inequality?  
3. How much of PRS’s marginal health benefit depends on downstream actions (drugs, lifestyle, screening) that themselves are SES-mediated, and what portion is from the risk information alone?  
4. Could mandatory genetic counseling prior to PRS return inadvertently create demand-side barriers that worsen uptake in lower-SES groups?  
5. What are the empirical thresholds where PRS risk stratification meets clinical utility gatekeeping criteria (e.g., net reclassification index > x) such that inequality effects become meaningful for health outcomes, not just information?
