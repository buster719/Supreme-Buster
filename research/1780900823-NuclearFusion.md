### Why This Domain Matters
- **Civilisation expansion**: Controlled fusion promises dense, carbon-free baseload energy with abundant fuel (deuterium from seawater, lithium for tritium breeding). It directly addresses long-term energy security, deep decarbonisation, and the energy scale needed for space exploration, large‑scale desalination, or atmospheric carbon removal.  
- **Truth-seeking**: Fusion forces us to understand high‑temperature plasma physics, electromagnetism, materials under extreme neutron fluxes, and the quantum/nuclear interactions that govern energy release. Progress requires integrating theory, simulation, and mega‑engineering experiments — a prime testbed for the scientific method.  

### Current Frontier Questions
1. **Plasma confinement and stability**: How can we maintain a burning plasma (Q ≥ 1) for economically relevant durations without disruptions? Key issues: ELMs, sawtooth instabilities, turbulent transport, and magnetic reconnection.  
2. **Tritium breeding and fuel cycle**: The capital deuterium‑tritium (D‑T) reaction needs tritium, which does not exist naturally in quantity. Can a reactor breed enough tritium in lithium blankets while keeping the fuel cycle safe and efficient?  
3. **Materials and first‑wall survivability**: Under 14 MeV neutron bombardment, what materials can withstand severe displacement damage, helium/hydrogen embrittlement, and activation over component lifetimes?  
4. **Alternative concepts vs. tokamaks**: Can stellarators (e.g. Wendelstein 7‑X), spherical tokamaks, inertial confinement (NIF, IFE), or private approaches (CFS, TAE, Helion) leapfrog the mainstream ITER‑based path? What physics or engineering trade‑offs ultimately win?  
5. **Engineering scalability and economic viability**: Even if net energy is achieved, can fusion plants be built, maintained, and operated at a cost competitive with fission, renewables + storage, or deep geothermal?
