# Research-Chain Note: TMR & Checkpoint-Rollback Failure Thresholds Under TID/SEE

---

## 1. Follow-up Questions

1. At what cross-over SEU rate does triple-modular redundancy (TMR) transition from masking errors to amplifying them through correlated faults, and does this threshold differ significantly between FPGA-based TMR (TMRG toolsets) and ASIC implementations on identical process nodes?
2. How does checkpoint-rollback recovery degrade when the rollback window itself contains uncorrectable multi-bit upsets—i.e., what is the empirically measured mean-recovery-failure rate as a function of SEU flux for spacecraft memory subsystems (e.g., SDRAM with EDAC)?
3. What is the documented TID accumulation point at which watchdog-timer and lockstep-core recovery strategies on RAD750 or LEON3-class processors lose guaranteed recovery liveness due to increasing latchup frequency?
4. Are there published fault-injection experiments that systematically degrade TMR voter reliability by increasing bit-flip correlation across redundant channels, and what correlation coefficient roughly marks the failure cliff?
5. How do adaptive redundancy schemes (variable TMR, selective hardening, checkpoint frequency scaling) compare to static TMR in empirical SEU-fault-injection campaigns—do they extend the operational envelope meaningfully or just shift the failure mode?
