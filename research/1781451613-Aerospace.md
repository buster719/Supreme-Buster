## 1. Follow-up Questions

1. What specific shielding configurations (e.g., Whipple bumper, multi-shock, stuffed Whipple) are currently integrated into or required around active docking port surrounds on ISS, and how does their mass/geometry constrain the usable capture envelope?
2. Do any current or planned deep-space docking standards (IDSS/IDSS-NG, APAS, NDS/NASA Docking System) include explicit MMOD shielding zones in their mechanical interface control documents (ICDs), or is shielding treated as a spacecraft-level envelope that the docking mechanism must fit within?
3. What sealing redundancy requirements exist for docking port hatches and active seals after a hypervelocity impact event in the vicinity of the seal surface—do standards require post-impact seal verification capability?
4. How does the transition from LEO debris environment to deep-space micrometeoroid flux profiles change the design optimization for docking-area protection (velocity distributions, particle size spectra, flux rates)?
5. Have any test programs (e.g., NASA HVIT, ESA hypervelocity facilities) specifically tested MMOD impacts on representative docking interface hardware, and what were the failure modes?
